# Neo-brutalist website study: 30 references

Researched 2026-10-11. Output: [proposed design system](../../DESIGN.md).

## Scope and evidence

The request was for 30 of the most popular neo-brutalist websites. There is no substantiated popularity ranking in the sources examined. This report therefore uses recognizable brands, award/gallery recognition, and explicit designer attribution as discovery signals, **not traffic estimates**. Smaller community references are labeled as such. It does not claim these are the world's top 30 sites.

The investigation screened dozens of candidates because many published neo-brutalist examples have changed. The main comparison contains **30 neo-brutalist and explicitly labeled graphic/retro hybrids: 28 current sites and two historical designs**. It combines recognizable brands with smaller attributed examples; recognition is not equal across the set. Seven adjacent designs are retained separately for editorial and dither lessons.

For current sites, primary pages were read, then rendered in headless Chrome at 1440×1050. Screenshots were actually inspected. For the two historical sites, the gallery-preserved screenshot was inspected instead: attempts to render the archived HTML failed DNS. Historical examples establish composition only, not current behavior, responsive performance or loaded font families. Six references were also inspected at a true emulated 390×844 viewport: Gumroad, Gitingest, Code in Public, FlowFest, Sui Overflow, and neobrutalism.dev. Eight additional captures examine lower-page or overlay states. These are a visual sample, not a full accessibility, interaction, or performance audit. A visible loading screen or popup is reported as a limitation, not treated as the whole design. No site-wide font or animation claim is inferred from a single still.

Initial captures reflect the browser's rendered state, including dark surfaces and banners; they are not necessarily each site's default state on every device. Exact type families in the evidence manifest are computed-style declarations, not proof that every declared font loaded successfully. Proposed tokens in DESIGN.md are our decisions, not sampled brand specifications.

Discovery sources include the [NeoBrutalism showcase](https://neobrutalism.com/showcase), [Curated's style listings](https://curated.design/inspiration/), and award records linked below. A gallery inclusion establishes editorial recognition only. Six initial candidates shared one component-kit lineage; that is correlated evidence, not six independent endorsements of the same UI.

Sticky section labels come from Ethan's earlier Simon Austerberry preference. They were not measured as a common behavior of this corpus. Likewise the proposed red/black/paper palette comes from Ethan's own exploration; it is not a statistical average of these websites.

## What explains the generic feeling of the existing prototype

This is an interpretation of the current local study, not a user-research finding. Its large stock persona heading, equal-weight question rows, repeated outlined sections, and decorative tree mark carry most of its character. There is little visual material that belongs specifically to Ethan. Changing green to red or increasing border thickness would leave that underlying composition intact.

The alternative is to design around his name, concise confirmed context, a selected image treatment, genuine writing, and genuine work. A ruled article list, one artifact feature, and a short about section should have different shapes because they do different jobs. Repetition belongs in spacing and typography, not in forcing everything into one card.

These do not prevent writing a concrete proposed system, but a document alone cannot establish that the final composition feels right.
